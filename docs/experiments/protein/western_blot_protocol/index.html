    Basic Western Blot Protocol¶

    This is a basic protocol for chemiluminescent detection of protein transferred to a nitrocellulose membrane, using HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies.

    A. Electrophoresis¶

    1. Use Bio-Rad Mini-PROTEAN TGX gel(s) for good results with up to 15 wells. You can also use a Bio-Rad Criterion gel for up to 26 wells.
    2. Remove the strip from the bottom of the gel and carefully remove the comb (it is easier to do this before the gel is clamped into the tank cassette).
    3. Assemble the cassette by sandwiching the gel(s) between the gasket and the outer edge. If you only have one gel, put a plastic buffer dam on the other side. Do not accidentally throw this buffer dam away when finished!
    4. Lock the gel(s) into the cassette by closing the plastic flanges.
    5. Put the cassette in the tank, then fill the top of the gel and the bottom of the tank with 1X SDS-PAGE buffer.
    6. Use a syringe and needle to rinse out each well with ~1 mL of 1X SDS-PAGE buffer.
    7. Load normalized amounts of cellular lysate in Laemmli buffer, or other appropriate protein samples, into each well. Keep the sample volume consistent to preserve the same ionic strength across wells, and fill any empty wells with the same volume of 1X Laemmli buffer.
    8. Run at a constant 200 V for approximately 35 minutes, until the dye front is at the bottom of the gel.

    B. Transfer (Turbo semi-dry transfer protocol)¶

    1. When electrophoresis is nearly finished, for each gel to be transferred, wet 2× transfer stacks or sheets of thick filter paper, and 1× nitrocellulose membrane, with cold 1X Turbo transfer buffer. Important! Do not pour buffer directly onto dry paper and membranes, which can unevenly wet them and lead to poor transfer. Instead, place them gently on top of the buffer for a few minutes, allowing them to absorb the buffer via capillary action.
    2. Disassemble the gel by carefully prying apart the plastic plates with a gel opening tool. For best results, crack open the sides of the gel, then lift the top plate off like a hinge, from the bottom. Do not equilibrate the gel in 1X Turbo transfer buffer.
    3. If the gel is a stain-free gel, you may capture a picture of the protein content using a stain-free imaging protocol on a GelDoc.
    4. Assemble a "sandwich" on the bottom electrode, in the following order from Bottom to Top: Paper - Membrane - Gel - Paper. Pour a few drops of buffer onto the membrane before placing the gel, so you can move the gel around a little if needed.
    5. Use a roller or a glass pipet to roll all bubbles out, with several firm rolls from the middle to the edge, in all directions.
    6. Press the top electrode onto the sandwich and lock it.
    7. For best resolution, run the standard transfer program (1 A, 20 V, 30 minutes) to transfer proteins to the membrane. For faster results, use the Turbo transfer program (1.2 A, 25 V, 7 minutes).
    8. Disassemble the Turbo transfer apparatus and the sandwich, and inspect the membrane to make sure the ladder has transferred.

    C. Ponceau stain (if you did not capture a stain-free image)¶

    1. Place the membrane in a plastic tray and rinse with MilliQ water for a few minutes (this removes residual SDS, which interferes with Ponceau staining).
    2. Remove the water, add 10-20 mL Ponceau stain per membrane, and shake for 5 minutes at room temperature.
    3. Remove the Ponceau stain. Do not pour it down the sink! Keep it in a dedicated waste container, from which it can be reused several times.
    4. Add ~20 mL MilliQ water to destain, and shake for a few minutes. Repeat once or twice, until the nitrocellulose membrane background is white (water washes can be poured down the sink).
    5. Take a picture of the transferred, stained proteins on a GelDoc.
    6. If you will cut the membrane with a razor blade to probe different parts of it with different antibodies, do so now.
    7. Remove the Ponceau stain from the membrane(s) with 2-3 washes in 5-15 mL TBST, changing every few minutes.

    D. Block membrane¶

    1. While (de)staining the membrane, prepare 40 mL of 5% milk by dissolving 2 g skim milk in 40 mL TBST. Shake or vortex to dissolve. Any bubbles will quickly dissipate.
    2. Move the membrane(s) to the tray(s) in which the primary antibody incubation will be performed. This is important, because milk also helps to "block" the tray itself (preventing antibody binding).
    3. Pour 2-5 mL of milk into the tray to cover the membrane. Do not pour liquid directly onto the membrane, which could damage it or dislodge proteins. Instead, pour liquid onto the side of the tray, and let it flow gently onto the membrane.
    4. Block the membrane for at least 30 minutes, and up to 4 hours, at room temperature.

    E. Primary antibody incubation¶

    1. Dilute the primary antibody in 5% milk in TBST, at the dilution listed in Validated antibodies. If unsure, perform a titration, or use 1:5,000 as a good starting dilution. Prepare ~5 mL per membrane (for example, 5 μL antibody in 5 mL milk, to make a 1:1,000 dilution).
    2. Drain the blocking solution and add the diluted primary antibody to the side of the tray.
    3. Incubate overnight at 4 °C with gentle shaking.
    4. Drain the primary antibody back into a labeled 15-mL conical and store at 4 °C. Diluted primary antibodies can be reused several times.

    F. Secondary antibody incubation and development¶

    1. Wash each membrane 1× with ~5 mL TBST, fast shaking, for ~10 seconds.
    2. Wash each membrane 3× with ~5 mL TBST, slow shaking, for 3 minutes each wash.
    3. Dilute the HRP-conjugated secondary antibody 1:20,000 in TBST (for example, 2 μL in 40 mL TBST). Use goat anti-rabbit (GαR) HRP for rabbit primaries and goat anti-mouse (GαM) HRP for mouse primaries.
    4. Drain the membranes and rinse 1-2× with a few mL of the appropriate secondary. Then add ~5 mL and incubate for 20 minutes at room temperature with slow shaking.
    5. Repeat the TBST washes (steps 1-2).
    6. Prepare SuperSignal West Pico substrate by combining equal volumes of each component. For weak signals (for example, ATF4 or phosphoproteins), mix in SuperSignal West Femto: for 1:10 Femto:Pico, combine 9 mL Pico substrate with 0.5 mL of each Femto component.
    7. Drain the membranes, blot briefly dry with a Kimwipe, and add 2.5-5 mL of substrate. Incubate for 5 minutes at room temperature with slow shaking.
    8. (At the GelDoc) Drain the membranes, blot briefly dry, sandwich between two plastic sheets, and roll flat. Expose for 2-120 seconds, depending on signal strength.

    G. Strip and reprobe¶

    Stripping is necessary when reprobing with a primary antibody from the same host species, or for a protein of similar size to the original target. If planning to strip the membrane, first probe for the weakest signal, then strip and reprobe for a stronger signal. For example, probe for a low abundance protein before stripping and reprobing for an abundant loading control.

    Component Volume
    Water to 50 mL
    10% glycine-HCl, pH 2.2 5 mL
    10% SDS 0.5 mL
    10% Tween-20 0.5 mL
    1. Prepare mild stripping buffer according to the table above.
    2. Rinse the membrane in a few mL of MilliQ water for a few minutes.
    3. Add ~5-10 mL of mild stripping buffer and shake for 15-30 minutes at room temperature.
    4. Drain and wash 3× with ~7 mL TBST, for 3 minutes each wash.
    5. Block for at least 30 minutes at room temperature (see D. Block membrane).
    6. Continue with E. Primary antibody incubation.

    Notes¶

    • The primary incubation step can be as short as 30 minutes at room temperature for some antibodies. However, incubating overnight at 4 °C is the standard practice.
    • If probing for phosphorylated proteins, block the membrane and dilute antibodies in 5% BSA instead of milk.
    • If using fluorescent secondary antibodies for detection (instead of chemiluminescent HRP), block with a commercial buffer (for example, Bio-Rad EveryBlot) instead of milk for best results. Also perform washes and secondary antibody incubation using 1X TBS (without Tween).

    Bio-Rad Criterion 8.7 13.3
